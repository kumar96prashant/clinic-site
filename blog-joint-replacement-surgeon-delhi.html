When is Joint Replacement Necessary?

Joint replacement is usually considered when conservative treatments—such as medications, physical therapy, and injections—no longer provide relief. You may be a candidate for surgery if you experience:

  • Severe knee or hip pain that limits everyday activities (walking, bending, climbing stairs).
  • Moderate-to-severe pain while resting, either day or night.
  • Chronic joint inflammation and swelling that doesn't improve with medication.
  • Joint deformity, such as a bowing in or out of your knee.

Life After Surgery

The vast majority of patients experience a dramatic reduction in joint pain and a significant improvement in their ability to perform daily activities. While high-impact sports may be discouraged, activities like walking, swimming, golfing, and cycling are highly encouraged to maintain the health of your new joint.
